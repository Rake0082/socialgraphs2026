Research question

Is the Marvel Wikipedia network hub-dominated — i.e. does its degree distribution have a heavy (power-law-like) tail rather than a light (exponential) one — and do the in-degree and out-degree distributions differ in a way that tells us how the network was built?

Method

  • Data. Week-1 frozen snapshot from the course data page: 303 character nodes, 1 784 directed edges (article A links to article B). Loaded with every node added first, so the 17 isolates survive.
  • Tools. NetworkX for the graph; in-degree / out-degree; weakly and strongly connected components; degree distributions plotted against k + 1 on linear and log–log axes, with logarithmic binning (width-1 bins to k + 1 = 7, doubling bins after).
  • Choices made. Directed graph kept directed for the in/out analysis; the k + 1 shift keeps degree-0 nodes on the log axis; binning density is count / (N × bin width).
  • Reproduce. Notebook: week1_distribution.ipynb.

What surprised us

  • Out-degree has a ceiling, in-degree does not. The busiest article links to ~28 other characters; the most-linked-to character is referenced by 106. An out-link is written by one page's editors (bounded by article length); an in-link is conferred by everyone else (unbounded, rich-get-richer).
  • 58 of 303 characters have in-degree 0 — nobody links to them — yet almost all of them still link outward.
  • 1 784 directed edges collapse to exactly 1 434 undirected because 350 pairs of characters link to each other, so ⟨k⟩ = 2m/n ≈ 9.5.
  • The tail is too heavy for an exponential. Under an exponential with the same mean, seeing any character at k = 106 is astronomically unlikely — but with n = 303 we cannot pin down the exact shape by eye.

Stretch goal — how reproducible is the dataset?

We rebuilt the network from the live MediaWiki API and compared it to the snapshot. The category has drifted from 303 to 380 pages since the 2026-08-26 freeze (3 removed, 80 added). For the 15 highest out-degree pages our body-text link rule recovered 92.8 % of the snapshot's edges with 100 % precision — the misses are almost all in-prose character tables, which our HTML filter strips.
